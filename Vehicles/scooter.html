ELECTRIC vs NON-ELECTRIC SCOOTERS

Feature Electric Scooters Non-Electric Scooters
Performance High torque, suitable for urban commuting. Higher speed, suitable for long-distance travel.
Environmental Impact Zero emissions, eco-friendly. Produces emissions, less eco-friendly.
Cost Higher upfront cost, but lower running cost. Lower upfront cost, higher fuel cost.
Maintenance Low maintenance due to fewer moving parts. Regular maintenance needed for engine and parts.
Fuel Powered by electricity, needs charging. Runs on petrol or diesel, needs refueling.
Range Can travel distances between 10–50 miles (16–80 km) on a single charge, depending on battery size, terrain, and weight. The range depends on the rider's endurance, as it’s powered entirely by leg effort.
Safety Can reach higher speeds, making accidents potentially more dangerous if not careful, especially if riders aren’t wearing safety gear. Slower speeds generally make them less risky, but balance and control are essential, especially for beginners.
Battery Life Battery life degrades over time, meaning you’ll eventually need to replace the battery (typically after 1-3 years). No battery to worry about, so no concerns over battery degradation.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How often should I service my scooter?

It is recommended to service your scooter every 3,000-5,000 km or as advised in the owner's manual. Regular servicing ensures better performance and longevity.

What type of fuel should I use for my scooter?

Most scooters run on unleaded petrol. Check your scooter's manual for the recommended fuel type to ensure optimal performance.

Why is my scooter not starting?

Common reasons include a dead battery, clogged carburetor, or fuel issues. Check the battery and fuel levels before troubleshooting further.

How can I improve my scooter’s mileage?

Maintain proper tire pressure, avoid sudden acceleration, and get regular servicing to maximize fuel efficiency.
