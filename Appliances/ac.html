EXPLORE BY CATEGORY

Split AC

A split air conditioner consists of two main units: an indoor unit, which is placed inside the room, and an outdoor unit, which is installed outside the building. The indoor unit is responsible for cooling the air, while the outdoor unit houses the compressor, which helps expel heat. Split ACs are popular for their quiet operation, energy efficiency, and ability to provide effective cooling for individual rooms or space.

Window AC

A window air conditioner is a compact cooling unit designed to fit into a window frame. It houses all the components, including the compressor, evaporator, and condenser, in a single unit. The unit cools the air by drawing warm air from the room, passing it over a cooling coil, and then releasing the chilled air back into the room. Window ACs are generally more affordable and easier to install compared to split units, but they can be noisier.

Portable AC

A portable air conditioner is a self-contained unit that can be moved easily from room to room. It typically features a hose that vents hot air out through a window or sliding door. These units combine cooling, dehumidifying, and fan functions in a single device, making them a versatile option for temporary or supplementary cooling. Portable ACs are ideal for small spaces or areas where permanent installations like window or split units are not feasible.

Inverter AC

An inverter air conditioner uses a variable-speed compressor that adjusts its speed to maintain the desired temperature, making it more energy-efficient than traditional AC units. Unlike conventional models that turn the compressor on and off, inverter ACs continuously adjust the cooling output, resulting in less energy consumption and more consistent cooling. This type of air conditioner also operates more quietly and can cool the room faster. Inverter ACs are often considered a long-term investment due to their lower operational costs and better performance.

Cassette AC

A cassette air conditioner is a type of split AC that is installed in the ceiling, making it ideal for spaces where floor or wall-mounted units are impractical. It features a square or circular grille that disperses cool air evenly in all directions, providing more uniform cooling throughout the room. Cassette ACs are commonly used in commercial spaces like offices, retail stores, and hotels due to their discreet installation and ability to cool large areas. They are also quieter than many other AC types, as most components are hidden within the ceiling.

Ducted AC

A ducted air conditioning system is a central cooling solution that uses a network of ducts to distribute cooled air throughout a building. It consists of an indoor unit, typically placed in the ceiling or basement, and an outdoor unit that houses the compressor. Ducted ACs offer efficient, even cooling for large spaces, as they can be connected to multiple rooms or zones. They are often chosen for their discreet installation, as the ducts are hidden within walls or ceilings, making them ideal for both residential and commercial properties.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

How do I clean my air conditioner filter?

Turn off the AC unit, carefully remove the filter, and rinse it under running water. Let it air-dry completely before placing it back in the unit.

Why is my air conditioner not cooling properly?

This could be due to a dirty filter, low refrigerant levels, or a clogged condenser coil. Regular maintenance or professional servicing can resolve this issue.

What should I do if water is leaking from my air conditioner?

Check for a clogged drain pipe or a frozen evaporator coil. Clean the drain pipe and ensure proper airflow to prevent freezing.

How often should I service my air conditioner?

It's recommended to service your air conditioner at least once a year, ideally before the summer season, to ensure optimal performance and efficiency.

Can I install my air conditioner myself?

While it’s possible, professional installation is highly recommended to ensure proper setup, avoid damage, and validate warranty requirements.
