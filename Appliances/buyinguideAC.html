Buying Guide for Air Conditioner

When buying an air conditioner (AC), it's important to consider several factors to ensure you get the right unit for your needs.


  • Types of Air Conditioners: Checking the types of air conditioners is essential because different types of AC units serve different needs based on your space, installation preferences, and cooling requirements.

  • Room Size: Calculate the room size (length x width x height) to determine the cooling capacity you need. The cooling capacity is usually measured in BTUs (British Thermal Units). Room Size to BTU: 100-150 sq. ft: 5,000–6,000 BTU 150-250 sq. ft: 6,000–8,000 BTU 250-350 sq. ft: 8,000–10,000 BTU 350-450 sq. ft: 10,000–12,000 BTU 450-550 sq. ft: 12,000–14,000 BTU Larger rooms (500+ sq. ft): Consider a larger BTU rating or multiple units.

  • Energy Efficiency:Look for an Energy Efficiency Ratio (EER) or Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio (SEER). A higher EER/SEER means better energy efficiency. Consider Energy Star-rated models, which use 10–50% less energy than standard models, reducing electricity bills in the long term. Inverter ACs are also more energy-efficient since they adjust the compressor speed, saving power when cooling is not needed at full capacity.

  • Cooling Power (BTUs): Choose a unit with an appropriate BTU for your room size. Too small an AC will struggle to cool, while too large a unit will waste energy. For larger rooms, you may need a split AC or central air system, while smaller rooms can use window or portable AC units.

  • Noise Levels: Check the noise level in decibels (dB). Split and inverter ACs tend to be quieter than window or portable units. The noise level of a typical window or portable AC is around 50–60 dB, while split units are usually quieter (40–50 dB).

  • Installation and Maintenance: Window ACs are relatively easy to install, but may require assistance if the window is non-standard. Split and Central ACs require professional installation, which can add to the overall cost. Choose a model with easy-to-clean filters and maintenance needs to ensure long-term performance

  • Brand and Reviews: CResearch reputable brands like Daikin, LG, Samsung, Voltas, Hitachi, and Carrier, which are known for reliable products. Check online reviews to gauge the performance and reliability of the unit

  • Price: Initial Cost: Window ACs tend to be the least expensive, while split and inverter ACs are pricier due to their higher efficiency and advanced features. Operating Cost: Consider the electricity costs associated with running your AC. Units with higher energy efficiency will reduce running costs. Warranty: Opt for an AC with a good warranty to ensure peace of mind for repairs and service.

By evaluating these factors carefully, you'll be able to select the right air conditioner for your needs, providing both comfort and efficiency in the long run.
